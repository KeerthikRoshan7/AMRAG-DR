# Follow-Up Intervals and Referral Guidance for Diabetic Retinopathy
Source: American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) Preferred Practice Pattern
(PPP) for Diabetic Retinopathy; ICO/ADA screening guidance. (Paraphrased
summary for internal knowledge-base use -- consult the current official AAO
PPP document for authoritative, up-to-date clinical guidance.)

## Screening initiation
  - Type 1 diabetes: first dilated eye exam ~5 years after diagnosis.
  - Type 2 diabetes: first dilated eye exam at time of diagnosis (retinopathy
    may already be present given typically delayed T2D diagnosis).
  - Pregnant patients with pre-existing diabetes: examine promptly after
    conception; follow every 3-12 months depending on severity, since
    pregnancy can accelerate progression. Gestational diabetes alone does
    not require a dedicated eye exam.

## Recommended follow-up interval by severity (routine, non-pregnant adult)
  - No DR or minimal/mild NPDR, no DME: re-examine in ~12 months
    (some protocols suggest 12-24 months for true no-DR cases).
  - Moderate NPDR: follow-up in an interval shorter than 12 months --
    commonly cited as every 6-9 months, more frequent in populations with
    documented faster progression.
  - Severe NPDR (or worse) or any clinically significant DME: refer to an
    eye care professional / retina specialist for evaluation and likely
    treatment; follow-up commonly every 1-3 months until stabilized.
  - PDR (non-high-risk or high-risk): urgent referral; treatment (panretinal
    photocoagulation and/or anti-VEGF) typically initiated promptly,
    especially for high-risk features.

## Referral triage summary (useful as an agent decision table)
  - No DR / Mild NPDR without DME -> routine annual screening, no referral
    to specialist required.
  - Moderate NPDR without DME -> referral appropriate but not urgent;
    shortened routine interval.
  - Severe NPDR, any PDR, or DME threatening/involving the fovea ->
    refer to ophthalmologist/retina specialist; treat as time-sensitive.
  - Vitreous hemorrhage, tractional retinal detachment, rubeosis, or
    neovascular glaucoma -> same-day / emergent ophthalmic referral.

## Modifying factors
  - Rapid, tight glycemic control (including with newer agents such as
    semaglutide) has been associated with an early, transient worsening of
    retinopathy in some patients -- closer monitoring is reasonable after
    starting aggressive glucose-lowering therapy.
  - Puberty is associated with accelerated retinopathy progression and may
    warrant closer follow-up in adolescent patients.
  - Blood pressure and HbA1c control remain the two most important
    modifiable systemic factors for reducing DR onset/progression risk.

## Why referral timing matters clinically
Baseline DR severity is itself the single strongest predictor of who
progresses to sight-threatening disease -- pooled cohort data show roughly
a 2.7x higher 4-year hazard of progression to PDR for moderate vs. mild
NPDR, and roughly 6.5x higher for severe vs. mild NPDR. This is the clinical
rationale behind shortening the follow-up interval as severity increases
rather than using a single fixed annual interval for all patients: the
window in which severe NPDR can silently progress to vision-threatening
PDR is measured in months, not years, in a meaningful fraction of patients.

## Referral urgency reference table (for a decision-support agent)
| Finding | Suggested action |
|---|---|
| No DR, no DME | Routine screening in 12 months (or up to 24 months in select low-risk protocols) |
| Mild NPDR, no DME | Routine screening in ~12 months |
| Moderate NPDR, no DME | Shortened interval, ~6-9 months; referral appropriate but not urgent |
| Severe NPDR (any 4-2-1 criterion met), no DME | Refer to ophthalmologist/retina specialist; re-check in 1-3 months |
| Any PDR (non-high-risk) | Refer promptly; treatment planning (PRP and/or anti-VEGF) |
| High-risk PDR (NVD >= ~1/4-1/3 disc area, or any NV + vitreous/preretinal hemorrhage) | Urgent referral; treatment typically initiated promptly |
| Center-involving DME (any severity level) | Refer for anti-VEGF evaluation regardless of NPDR/PDR grade |
| Vitreous hemorrhage, tractional retinal detachment, rubeosis, neovascular glaucoma | Same-day / emergent referral |
